When you work in a large organization it can become frustrating when it seems that a group or a person you depend on isn't delivering on what you expect they should. It is easy to focus your energy and anger toward this situation. This line of thinking, even if often justified, leads to more dissatisfaction than any other topic in an organization. The "why is that team always late?", or "why do we put up with that bozo?"

## What part of this can you control? 

While it can be easy to think everyone else is causing you problems. It can often be the case that the subject of your frustration has no idea you are frustrated by their actions. There isn't much you should expect to happen if you haven't given honest feedback based on your perspective of the topic. Giving critical feedback is difficult, but often necessary, and likely welcome. 

Giving feedback can be tricky. Your goal is to get the other person to hear your feedback, so if your approach causes them to become defensive your point is likely going to get lost. Start by ensuring you have a basis of trust. If the other person feels that they are in a safe situation it is much easier to be honest. Be clear that you likely don't see the whole picture. You likely don't know what is driving this other person's behavior. Assuming you know the whole story will likely not enable you to get off on the right path. 

You owe it to the other person to provide the feedback. But, you don't control if they do anything with this feedback, or if they will act on the feedback in a way that will produce the outcome you are hoping to accomplish. 

## Do you have your house in order? 

If you don't get the outcome you are looking for after providing feedback, what now?
