Sunday Dec 21st 2025, 6:41 AM

Learning how to write publicly has been one of my greater challenges. I've been resistant to putting my ideas together in a form that is difficult to take back or tune once the thought is out in the open. I don't think I have ever been good at it. Even in forums on topics I have a perspective on, I tend to hold back. It's not that I don't know how the internet works. If anything I know it too well to understand that no one ever won an argument on the internet.

But my goal with writing isn't to win arguments. Don't get me wrong, those that know me in person, know I love a good debate. But I'm not here to debate either. I want to use this platform to learn for myself how to better think about a topic and articulate in writing what I currently believe. It isn't to put anyone else down, or say their points of views are wrong. It is to develop my point of view, in the most educated way I can. I am sure I will change my mind over time, with new information. And admit when I was wrong. In person, I can read the room and fix a misunderstanding instantly. Online, I am flying blind.

I'm no pundit. Besides that it seems that in today's society there are too many people that are overly confident in their position. Especially online.
I'm also no expert. Sure there are topics I have extensive experience with. Like leading teams, building product, and helping grow leaders. But since I retired a couple years ago I am less and less interested in discussing these topics, save for a few close friends that I still work with in this fashion. So no one should be taking my point of view as the truth, whatever that is anymore.

My resistance to writing publicly goes back to grade school. English, grammar, spelling were my most dreaded subjects. I think I worked harder at avoiding the subjects than attempting to improve. Reading for pleasure wasn't something I started doing until I was an adult. And until then I didn't really know how common and important it was to read to young kids. It wasn't something that I had in my childhood. I told myself that I was an engineer. I'm just not good at that writing stuff.

Don't worry I have no aspirations of becoming a great writer. But what I realize as I read, was that writing has this amazing ability to help you think. I've been journaling for many years. It started over a decade ago when I was in a rather stressful role. I started writing on my phone while I commuted by bus into the office every day. Getting my thoughts down, questioning my assumptions, attempting to understand the motivations of others became a powerful tool to help me navigate the situation.

I still journal daily today. The stressful corporate job is gone but I still find the daily act of working through my thoughts helpful. The transition from a career to retired life is a whole other level challenge that I think is underappreciated. Finding meaning when every day is a blank page is not easy. Defining your day when for decades your schedule was often set by the priorities of others is a major shift, a crazy shift. But a change nonetheless.

Over the last two plus years in retirement I have started to set some direction for myself. Areas that I would like to develop in myself that I either never felt I had time to develop, or were areas that I actively avoided for one reason or another. Writing is one of these areas. I know no one is likely to read any of what I write here. But that isn't the point. I'm not trying to be a content creator. My goal is to push through my own resistance to prove my past self wrong. That no matter how old I am I can redefine who I am, if only to myself. Changing identities is difficult. The retirement transition [taught me that](https://markgroves.us/2024/12/04/who-are-you-after-your-identity-vanishes/). The measurement for me is if I am pushing myself through the resistance to try.

My level of discomfort is something I need to overcome. I have some ideas for topics I would like to explore. But like my private journal I am expecting the topics to vary greatly and likely be partial investigations in each post. But my goal is to treat this space as a long term conversation, if only with myself, in how I think about any given topic. I expect to evolve my thinking, change my mind with new information. If I can't evolve me thinking what's the point of learning?
