Learning how to write publicly has been one of my greatest challenges. I've been resistant to putting my ideas together in a form that is difficult to take back or adjust once the thought is out in the open. I don't think I have ever been good at it. Even in forums on topics I have a perspective on, I tend to hold back. It's not that I don't know how the internet works. If anything, I know it too well, understanding that no one has ever won an argument on the internet.

But my goal with writing isn't to win arguments. Those who know me in person know I love a good debate. But I'm not here to debate. I want to use this platform to learn how to think about a topic and articulate in writing what I currently believe. To develop my point of view in the most educated way I can. I am sure I will change my mind over time with new information and admit when I was wrong. In person, I can read the room and attempt to correct a misunderstanding instantly. Online, I am flying blind.

I'm no pundit. I don't know what I think on many topics. Besides that, it seems that in today's society, there are too many people who are overly confident in their position, especially online. I'm also no expert. So no one should take my point of view as the truth, whatever that is anymore.

My resistance to writing publicly goes back to grade school. English, grammar, and spelling were my most dreaded subjects. I think I worked harder at avoiding the subjects than attempting to improve. Reading for pleasure wasn't something I started doing until I was an adult. And until then, I didn't really know how common and important it was to read. It wasn't something that I had in my childhood. I told myself that I was an engineer. I'm just not good at that writing stuff.

I have no aspirations of becoming a great writer. But I've realized that  writing has this amazing ability to help you think. I've been journaling for many years. It started over a decade ago when I was in a rather stressful role. I started writing on my phone while I commuted by bus into the office every day. Getting my thoughts down, questioning my assumptions, and attempting to understand the motivations of others became a powerful tool to help me navigate the situation.

I still journal daily today. The stressful corporate job is gone, but I still find the daily act of working through my thoughts helpful. The transition from a career to retired life is a whole other level of challenge that I underappreciated. Finding meaning when every day is a blank page is a new world to me. Part of my journaling practice has been working through these challenges. But journaling is a private act. No one else sees what I write. It's a conversation with myself. 

My level of discomfort is something I need to overcome. I have some ideas for topics I would like to explore. But like my private journal, I expect the topics to vary greatly and likely be partial investigations in each post. But my goal is to treat this space as a long-term conversation, if only with myself, in how I think. I expect to evolve my thinking and change my mind with new information. If I can't evolve my thinking, what's the point?
